Technique 01 · Kinetic headline

SCROLL STORIES BUILT RIGHT.

Every effect below is rebuilt from scratch with Lenis + GSAP ScrollTrigger — the same recipe the Wero site uses. Scroll down and read the red notes to see how each one works.

How the headline works: each word sits inside a .line-mask with overflow:hidden. On load, GSAP animates the words from yPercent:100 (hidden below the mask) up to 0, staggered — so they rise into view one after another. This is the poor-man's version of GSAP's paid SplitText plugin.

Technique 02 · Reveal on scroll

Things appear
as they enter.

How it works: each card starts at opacity:0; translateY(40px) (the .reveal class). A single ScrollTrigger.batch() watches them and, when each scrolls into view, tweens it to its natural position with a stagger. Batching = one observer for all cards instead of one per card (cheap + smooth). This is the workhorse effect — you'll use it more than any other.

Technique 03 · Pin & scrub

The signature move.

How it works: the tall .pin-wrap (300vh) gives us scroll distance. The stage is position:sticky so it "sticks" while you scroll past that distance. A GSAP timeline with scrub:true is tied to that scroll range, so the animation plays forward and backward with your scroll instead of on a clock. Here it crossfades Phase 1 → Phase 2 and fills the progress bar. This is the Wero site's core "scroll and discover" mechanic.

Technique 04 · Parallax depth

Layers move
at different speeds.

How it works: each floating block has a data-speed. One ScrollTrigger with scrub shifts every layer by speed × scroll on the Y axis. Positive = drifts down slower (feels far), negative = moves up faster (feels near). That speed difference is the entire illusion of depth.
Technique 05 · Grid stagger

Grids that

How it works: same ScrollTrigger.batch() as the cards, but with a small stagger and scale so the tiles pop in as a wave. This is how the Wero partner-logo wall animates. Placeholder tiles below — swap in your own partner logos.
