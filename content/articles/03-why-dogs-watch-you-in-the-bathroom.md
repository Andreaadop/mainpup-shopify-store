# Why Your Dog Watches You in the Bathroom (A Working Theory)

You know the moment. You close the bathroom door. There's a small pause. Then the soft, insistent scratching. Or the pointed sigh from the hallway. Or, if you didn't close the door in time, the intense unblinking eye contact from three feet away.

Your dog is watching you. Your dog is *always* watching you. And the bathroom, for reasons neither of you fully understand, is where the watching intensifies.

Here's what we know, what we suspect, and what your dog will not confirm.

## The three real reasons (approximately)

### 1. You're their pack, and packs don't split up

Dogs are descended from wolves. Wolves live in tight family groups that stay physically close, especially at vulnerable moments. And bathrooms — from a dog's perspective — are a vulnerable moment. You are stationary. You are somewhat exposed. You are focused on something that isn't the environment.

Your dog is not judging you. Your dog is on watch.

This is why some dogs will follow you into the bathroom, sit down, and then face *outward* toward the door. That's not affection. That's a shift.

### 2. Resource guarding — of you

To your dog, you are the best resource in the household. Food comes from you. Warmth comes from you. Belly rubs come from you. The idea that you might be alone somewhere, unmonitored, without them — is genuinely upsetting.

The bathroom, from this angle, is a door that closes between them and the source of everything. That's not a small thing for a species built around proximity.

### 3. Curiosity, plain and simple

Dogs have somewhere between 10,000 and 100,000 times more scent receptors than humans. The bathroom is, sensorially, one of the most information-dense rooms in your house. Every smell in there is data. Every sound is context. Your dog is not creepy — your dog is a research assistant.

---

## The absurdist theories (for completeness)

### 4. They believe you might drown

To a species that watches you go into a small tiled room with running water, close a door, and stay there for an inexplicable amount of time — the concern is not unreasonable. You are, from their perspective, taking a lot of risks.

Every time you emerge from the shower alive, you have exceeded their expectations.

### 5. Something happened in the bathroom once and they haven't gotten over it

You may not remember. Your dog does. There was a moment — a slipped foot, a loud pipe, a raised voice on a phone call — and your dog decided the bathroom required supervision from then on.

You will never know which incident. Your dog will never tell you.

### 6. In a previous life, they were a valet

We can't prove this. We can only observe.

### 7. They're checking that you're the same person you were before you closed the door

The scientific literature does not support this. But every dog owner has seen the look that a dog gives you when you come out of the bathroom, and it is unmistakably a diagnostic.

---

## What to do about it

Nothing. It's not a problem to solve. It's a small unpaid job your dog has taken on, largely without your input, for reasons rooted in 30,000 years of evolutionary partnership between our species.

Close the door if you need privacy. Leave it open if you don't mind the audience. Your dog will adapt. Your dog will also, at some point, sigh so audibly through the door that you'll open it just to see what they want. Which was, of course, the goal all along.
