# 100 Funny Dog Facts That Are Almost True

Your dog has a rich inner life. You're only occasionally invited into it.

Some of these facts are real. Some are observationally real — meaning every dog owner has seen this exact behavior and can't explain it. And some are, technically, made up by us. We'll let you sort out which is which. Your dog would prefer you don't ask.

_This is part of the [mainpups](/) field guide to pets and the humans who love them. If you finish this and start suspecting your dog was a chef in a former life, [we made a quiz for that](/quiz/)._

<h2 id="brain">Facts about your dog's brain (allegedly)</h2>

**1.** Your dog thinks in full sentences. They just choose not to share.

**2.** A dog's sense of smell is around 10,000 to 100,000 times more sensitive than a human's, which means your dog knows what you had for lunch yesterday and has formed opinions.

**3.** When your dog stares at you for a long time without moving, they are running a diagnostic.

**4.** Dogs dream. This has been confirmed by science. What they dream about is classified.

**5.** Your dog can recognize approximately 165 human words. They understand many more. They simply refuse to acknowledge most of them.

**6.** When your dog tilts their head, they are triangulating the source of a sound. When they tilt their head at *you*, they are triangulating the source of your confusion.

**7.** Dogs have episodic memory — they remember specific events. This means your dog remembers the one time you left them at the vet, and they've been keeping notes.

**8.** A dog's brain-to-body ratio is roughly that of a toddler. This does not, in fact, explain most of their behavior.

**9.** The reason your dog looks slightly disappointed when you come home an hour late is that their internal clock is more accurate than yours.

**10.** Dogs can be trained to detect diseases in humans by scent, including certain cancers. Your dog knows exactly how you're doing. They just can't get the vet to listen.

**11.** Your dog is not "stupid" for chasing their tail. They are auditioning for something you don't have the security clearance for.

**12.** Dogs experience jealousy. Studies confirm it. Your dog knows you were petting a different dog and they will need a moment to process.

<h2 id="past">Facts about your dog's mysterious past</h2>

**13.** Every dog was, at some point, something else.

**14.** The look your dog gives you when you take too long to open their food is called "the reckoning." It is also the look they used to give the merchants.

**15.** A tail wag is not always happy. Sometimes it's a warning. Sometimes it's memoir.

**16.** The sudden zoomies at 10:47 p.m. are not spontaneous. Your dog remembered something.

**17.** When your dog stops mid-walk and stares at nothing for 45 seconds, they are having a flashback. Do not intervene.

**18.** Your dog has favorite chairs the way other people have favorite people.

**19.** Every senior dog is, in some sense, retired. From what, we cannot say.

**20.** The reason your dog will only sleep at the foot of the bed and never at the head is a matter of security protocol.

**21.** Your dog's inexplicable fondness for one specific stuffed animal is a form of grief.

**22.** When your dog rolls in something disgusting, they are dressing for an occasion you are not invited to.

**23.** [The quiz](/quiz/) exists to help identify what your dog's past life actually was. We wouldn't have built it if the answer wasn't important.

**24.** Some dogs are simply older than they appear. This is not a joke and we don't have more to say about it.

<h2 id="food">Facts about food, snacks, and the sacred bag of treats</h2>

**25.** Your dog can hear a snack bag opening from up to two rooms away. Reinforced walls do not stop them.

**26.** The specific sound of a cheese wrapper is stored in a different part of your dog's brain than all other sounds.

**27.** When you drop food on the floor, your dog is already there. This is not fast reflexes. This is prophecy.

**28.** A dog can smell one drop of blood in 20 liters of water. They can smell a single piece of ham through a Tupperware container in the kitchen from the couch. This is not exaggeration.

**29.** Your dog's ability to look devastated when they see you eating something they cannot have is not learned. It is inherited.

**30.** Every dog has a distinct begging strategy. Some rely on stares. Some sit close. Some sigh audibly. The strategy is personality-tested and specific to them.

**31.** Dogs cannot eat chocolate, grapes, onions, or macadamia nuts. Please do not test any of this. Your dog knows and does not care.

**32.** The way your dog looks up while eating, checking that no one is going to take the bowl, is a tradition passed down.

**33.** When your dog leaves the last two pieces of kibble uneaten, they are making a point.

**34.** Dogs have taste buds — about 1,700 of them, compared to your 9,000. This means they have opinions, just not fully-formed ones.

**35.** Your dog is not "food motivated." Your dog is *committed* to food. There is a difference.

**36.** The reason your dog goes for the cardboard box before the toy that was inside it is because they know something about packaging that we don't.

**37.** Peanut butter is not medicine. Your dog just wants you to think so.

**38.** Your dog can, in fact, tell when you're saving them a piece of your dinner. They have been counting.

<h2 id="sleep">Facts about sleep, which is a serious commitment</h2>

**39.** Adult dogs sleep 12–14 hours a day. Puppies and seniors sleep even more. This is not laziness. This is scheduled maintenance.

**40.** Your dog's ability to fall asleep instantly, mid-thought, is a discipline honed over centuries.

**41.** Dogs have the same sleep cycles as humans — including REM, when they dream. Their little running motions are them working through unresolved narrative arcs.

**42.** If your dog sleeps with their belly up, they trust you completely. Or the room is too warm. Both are possibilities.

**43.** The dog who insists on sleeping under the covers is not cold. They are hiding.

**44.** The dog who sleeps in the doorway is not being difficult. They are on watch.

**45.** The dog who claims the exact center of your bed is announcing something about the hierarchy of the household.

**46.** Your dog's occasional muffled bark while asleep is a signal in a language you do not speak.

**47.** Dogs experience sleep paralysis sometimes. If your dog is twitching hard and looks distressed, wake them gently. They may have been dreaming about a specific incident.

**48.** The dog who sleeps in the smallest possible ball is descended from ancient burrow-dwellers. The dog who sprawls diagonally across every surface is descended from ancient monarchs.

**49.** The 3 p.m. nap is not optional. It is written into your dog's constitution.

**50.** Your dog has an assigned sleeping position on the couch. If you sit there, they will sit on you.

<h2 id="outdoors">Facts about the outdoor ritual</h2>

**51.** Your dog does not "go outside to use the bathroom." Your dog conducts a full outdoor inspection of which the bathroom is one small component.

**52.** The pre-poop spin is a survey. Your dog is checking the wind, the vibrations, the general vibe of the moment. Do not rush them.

**53.** Once the spot has been selected, your dog will look at you with dead-serious eye contact. This is not weird. This is love.

**54.** Sniffing is not procrastination. It's the dog equivalent of scrolling the group chat.

**55.** Your dog knows every dog that has passed through your neighborhood in the last 72 hours by scent alone. They have kept detailed mental files.

**56.** The reason your dog walks in a specific pattern is because that pattern is theirs.

**57.** When your dog stops and refuses to move mid-walk, they have received new information.

**58.** Your dog will greet a friendly stranger with more enthusiasm than they greeted your parents at Thanksgiving. This is not personal.

**59.** Rolling in grass immediately after being groomed is not defiance. It is a spiritual practice.

**60.** The reason your dog is deeply interested in a specific patch of dirt is because a squirrel once did something there that has never been repaid.

**61.** Every dog has one specific bush they must visit. If you deviate from the route, they will remind you.

**62.** Your dog's willingness to chase a ball is inversely proportional to how much money you spent on the ball.

<h2 id="otherdogs">Facts about other dogs, who they mostly tolerate</h2>

**63.** When your dog meets another dog, they exchange information. The information is: age, gender, mood, what they had for breakfast, and whether they mean any of this.

**64.** Your dog's opinion of small dogs is complicated and none of your business.

**65.** The friendly greeting between two dogs at a park is a diplomatic exchange. It could go either way at any moment.

**66.** Puppies are, to older dogs, deeply exhausting. Every senior dog has aged three years for every puppy they've had to babysit.

**67.** The dog park is not a social scene. It's a networking event.

**68.** Your dog has one specific dog friend they love unreasonably. They also have one specific dog they cannot tolerate. They will not explain either.

**69.** When two dogs run in circles together, they are participating in something old.

**70.** Your dog does not want you to pet other dogs. They will bring this up later.

<h2 id="you">Facts about you, from your dog's perspective</h2>

**71.** From your dog's point of view, you are a large, gentle, occasionally confusing being who provides food. This is enough.

**72.** Your dog knows exactly what mood you're in the moment you walk in the door. They will adjust their approach accordingly.

**73.** The way you talk to your dog when nobody else is around is exactly how your dog wishes you talked to them all the time.

**74.** Your dog thinks you should sleep more.

**75.** Your dog also thinks you should eat more slowly. And share.

**76.** When you cry, your dog shows up. Not to fix it. Just to be there. This is a technique they have practiced for approximately 30,000 years.

**77.** Your dog does not think you are boring. Your dog thinks you are the most interesting person in the world. This is a scientific fact and also a moral one.

**78.** Your dog does not understand your job. Your dog understands that your job is what takes you away, and your job is what brings you back. Your dog is neutral on the details.

**79.** Your dog would prefer if you did not close the bathroom door. [We wrote a whole thing about why](/blog/why-dogs-watch-you-in-the-bathroom).

**80.** The way your dog greets you when you come home from a five-day trip is exactly the same as when you come home from a five-minute trip.

**81.** Your dog is not judging you for what you're wearing. Your dog just wants to make sure it's really you under all that fabric.

<h2 id="enemies">Facts about household enemies (the vacuum, the mailman, the ceiling fan)</h2>

**82.** The vacuum cleaner is not a household appliance. It is a personal enemy. Your dog has been clear about this.

**83.** The mailman is not a friendly public servant. Your dog has been watching them for years and has a full dossier.

**84.** The Roomba is unforgivable and we don't need to explain further.

**85.** The ceiling fan is a persistent low-grade threat. Your dog is monitoring it. So far, so good.

**86.** The doorbell is a call to arms. Every doorbell. Even the doorbell in a commercial on TV. Your dog does not distinguish.

**87.** The one specific squirrel who taunts your dog through the window has been doing it for years. This is a rivalry.

**88.** Your dog's suspicion of the plumber, the electrician, and the delivery person is not xenophobia. It is quality control.

**89.** Fireworks are not celebratory. Fireworks are inexplicable and hostile and your dog would like the record to show that they warned you.

**90.** The sound of a car door closing outside the house is data. Your dog is processing.

<h2 id="history">Facts about dogs across history that we found on the internet</h2>

**91.** Dogs were domesticated somewhere between 15,000 and 40,000 years ago. Depending on which archaeologist you ask. Depending on how they're feeling that day.

**92.** The oldest known dog burial site is over 14,000 years old. The dog was buried with humans. Somebody loved this dog very much and it has been that long.

**93.** Ancient Egyptians shaved their eyebrows to mourn the death of their dogs. This should give you some sense of how seriously humans have always taken this.

**94.** There is a dog in the Bayeux Tapestry (11th century). His name is not recorded. We choose to believe it was something dignified.

**95.** Laika, a Soviet space dog, became the first living being to orbit Earth in 1957. She did not come home. There is a monument to her in Moscow.

**96.** Balto and Togo were sled dogs who helped deliver diphtheria antitoxin to Nome, Alaska in 1925. Togo did most of the work. Balto got the statue. This is dog politics.

**97.** The Basenji, one of the oldest known dog breeds, does not bark. It yodels. There is nothing else to add to this.

**98.** Newfoundland dogs have partially webbed feet and have been used as water rescue dogs for centuries. Yes, your dog could probably save your life. They just haven't had to yet.

<h2 id="unconfirmed">Facts your dog will not confirm</h2>

**99.** Your dog remembers you before you remember yourself. This has always been the arrangement.

**100.** You are your dog's whole life. Your dog is a chapter of yours. Somehow both are true, and neither of you seem to mind.
